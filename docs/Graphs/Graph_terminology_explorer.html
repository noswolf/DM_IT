What is a graph?

A graph G = (V, E) consists of a nonempty set V of vertices (or nodes) and a set E of edges. Each edge has one or two vertices attached to it, called its endpoints, and we say the edge connects its endpoints.

Loop
an edge that connects a vertex to itself.
Multiple edges
two or more edges that connect the same pair of vertices (also called parallel edges).
Directed edge (u, v)
an edge that starts at u and ends at v. It is drawn as an arrow.

Three questions name the type

  1. Are the edges undirected, directed, or both?
  2. Are multiple edges allowed?
  3. Are loops allowed?

Summary

Names nest. Every simple graph is also a multigraph (a multigraph may have multiple edges but doesn't have to), and every multigraph is also a pseudograph. When you classify a graph, give the most specific name.

Direction matters. In a directed graph, (u, v) and (v, u) are different edges. Having both of them does not count as multiple edges.

Bipartite challenge

A simple graph is bipartite when its vertices can be split into two disjoint sets V1 and V2 so that every edge joins a vertex of V1 to a vertex of V2. Tap a vertex to color it, tap again to switch color, and a third time to clear it. Can you 2-color each graph?

Theorem. A simple graph is bipartite if and only if its vertices can be colored with two colors so that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.

Equivalently, a graph is bipartite if and only if it has no cycle of odd length.
